Why I built this

My whole interest in Green Lanterns actually started with the live-action show, not the comics. I went in mostly curious and came out completely hooked — something about the concept just clicked for me. From there I went back and started reading the comics, caught up on the old movies, and worked my way through the animated shows too. The more I dug in, the more I realized how much history and how many versions of these characters there actually are, and I wanted a place to keep track of it all.

I've been reading Green Lantern comics on and off for years, and the Corps has always stood out to me for a simple reason: the ring runs on willpower, not luck or birthright. Anyone stubborn enough can wear one, and the stories are really about what a person does with that kind of responsibility. Hal, Guy, and Kyle all answer that question differently, and I wanted a place that put their answers side by side instead of scattered across wikis and old forum posts.

This project started as a way to practice HTML and CSS, and turned into an excuse to reread thirty years of back issues "for research."

What kept me motivated

Honestly, it was the small stuff. Getting Guy's stats table to line up correctly felt like a genuine win. Picking an accent color for each Lantern that matched their personality — steady green for Hal, hot crimson for Guy, cool blue for Kyle — turned a coding exercise into something that felt like mine. Every time I added a new detail I'd forgotten about (Guy's stint as a Red Lantern, Kyle carrying the entire Corps as its only member), the site got a little more useful, and that kept me coming back to it.

I also wanted proof, mostly to myself, that I could take a page from a blank file to something that actually looks put together: a shared color system, consistent typography, and pages that feel like they belong to the same site instead of three unrelated homework assignments. If you're browsing the character pages and the whole thing feels cohesive, that's the part I'm proudest of.
